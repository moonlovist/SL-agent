# Colour and Multi-band Knowledge

- A colour difference between central galaxy and surrounding structure can support distinct source populations.
- Blue arcs around a red galaxy are common lens candidates but are not unique to lensing; star-forming rings and arms can be blue.
- Comparable colours among multiple images can support a shared background source, subject to dust, PSF, and photometric uncertainties.
- Pairwise agreement is not automatically whole-system agreement. For a double, one reliable consistent edge connects the system; for three or more proposed images, high-confidence consistent edges must form a connected network spanning every required image. Requiring a connected spanning network avoids demanding every faint pair while preventing one convenient pair from certifying an otherwise untested group.
- A configuration is `group_consistent` only when that high-confidence network spans all required images and contains no reliable conflict. A consistent relation covering only a subset, or connectivity that depends on the suggestive tier, is `group_partially_consistent`. Reliable conflict mixed with reliable agreement is `group_mixed`; reliable conflict without agreement is `group_conflict`; otherwise the group is `group_unknown`.
- A quality-controlled relation that falls between consistency and strong conflict is retained as `group_colour_tension` when it is the only usable relation in a configuration. It can document why a morphologically probable system remains B, but it is not a reliable different-source result and cannot alone cap an independently secure A.
- Different source groups are evaluated separately. Segmentation fragments of one physical arc are merged or assigned to one physical-image hypothesis and must not be counted as independent multiple images.
- Group consistency is corroboration and cannot create or promote a lens grade by itself. A reliable conflict may cap A only when the pair belongs to a required supported configuration; partial or unknown coverage is neutral rather than negative.
- A reliable colour difference between a candidate structure and the central foreground can corroborate a distinct source population, but cannot create a lens configuration or determine A/B by itself. Similarity to the foreground is non-discriminating because residual foreground light and unrelated populations may share broad-band colours.
- At low signal-to-noise ratio, apparent colour differences or agreements may be unreliable. Any color conclusion must retain its measurement uncertainty and the available-band information.
- Finite low-S/N multiband fluxes may be retained as a continuous SED-shape diagnostic, but do not vote on the grade until that diagnostic is calibrated.
- Colour evidence is tiered rather than discarded at one threshold. Three common bands with S/N at least 3 form the high-confidence tier. Three bands with S/N at least 2.5, or two bands with S/N at least 3, form a suggestive tier that is recorded but cannot promote, veto, or cap a grade.
- Single-band data cannot measure colour. The agent must mark colour evidence unavailable rather than infer it from intensity.
