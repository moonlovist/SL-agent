# Lens Geometry Knowledge

- A plausible lens configuration has a measurable central mass/light centre and one or more structures arranged around it.
- Tangential alignment, a physically plausible radius envelope, and angular coverage are more discriminating together than any one quantity alone. Exact equal radii are not expected in general elliptical potentials, external shear, or asymmetric source configurations; radius similarity is supporting evidence rather than a universal gate.
- A single curved feature is compatible with lensing but is not sufficient: intrinsic arms, rings, interactions, and subtraction residuals remain alternatives.
- Several bright structures do not automatically form a double or quad lens system. Their positions must be checked relative to one common centre: components concentrated on the same side, widely separated incompatible radii, or an implausible trajectory weaken that particular multiple-image interpretation.
- Geometry alone remains incomplete when multiple images are suspected: compatible morphology, point-source/extended-source character, and color consistency are separate checks.
- Geometry derived from the same residual pixels is correlated and must be aggregated once.
- Missing redshift or mass-model agreement limits confirmation; the visual agent should report a candidate, not a physically confirmed lens.
